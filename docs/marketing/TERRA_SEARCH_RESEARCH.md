# MerchantCanvas Search-Landscape Research

**Research date:** 2026-07-28  
**Market and language used for planning:** English-language Shopify searches.  
**Method:** Live web-result review, official Shopify and Google documentation, and verified local product implementations. No search-volume, ranking, traffic, install, or review data was inferred.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. The MerchantCanvas product source and the verified app repositories establish what can be said about the products, pricing, limits, and availability.
2. Shopify documentation establishes platform behaviour, including Discount Functions and B2B draft orders.
3. Live result sets establish query framing and result-type patterns, not truth about a competing product's claims.
4. Community discussions identify language and recurring questions, but are not used as product evidence.

## Answer surfaces and user questions

The live result tool exposes ordinary result snippets. It does not reliably expose a user's Google AI Overview state, People Also Ask questions, geography, login state, or ad layout. Those fields are marked **not directly observable** rather than guessed.

The repeated question patterns are nevertheless clear from official results, app listings, and community discussions:

- What is a quantity break, and is it quantity-, product-, or cart-value based?
- Which products, variants, collections, customers, countries, dates, and combinations qualify?
- Will the same rule work in cart and checkout, and how does the shopper see progress?
- Is a quantity-break tool a better fit than a broad bundle, loyalty, or marketing suite?
- Can a B2B team keep quote context, approvals, buyer decisions, and draft-order conversion together?
- Does the system hide prices or collect generic quote requests, or does it structure internal approval?
- What happens after approval: draft order, invoice, payment, and order?
- What plan, price, trial, limit, and public install path are currently available?

## Result-type and positioning observations

### Tiered discounts and quantity breaks

- Shopify's current Discount Function documentation explicitly lists tiered discounts based on qualifying cart items, subtotal, and delivery requirements as a use case. It also lists B2B, cart, and checkout as supported surfaces, while the draft-order Admin surface is only partially supported. This is platform context, not a claim that every promotion shape works identically everywhere.
- Shopify's App Store collection for Function-based discount apps contains broad bundle, upsell, free-gift, and promotion products. That makes category breadth a poor differentiator on its own.
- Competitor snippets commonly combine quantity breaks, bundles, upsells, BOGO, gifts, and AOV claims. MerchantCanvas should differentiate by precise workflow scope, implementation evidence, fit guidance, clear package gates, and an absence of outcome guarantees.
- Community language repeatedly distinguishes a visible quantity table/progress cue from the checkout rule that decides eligibility. The guide and product page should preserve that distinction.

### B2B quotes and draft orders

- Shopify's B2B draft-order documentation shows that company/location context can carry pricing, payment terms, and checkout options into a draft order. It also documents negotiated custom prices, PO numbers, and invoices.
- Competing quote-app results often promise storefront request forms, price hiding, buyer portals, negotiation, PDF proposals, and order conversion. Those claims describe a different or broader category than an internal approval desk.
- MerchantCanvas should own the narrower language: create or receive, evaluate, approve or revise, share, and convert an agreed quote into a Shopify draft order. It should not promise ERP synchronization, customer-account portals, payment processing, public install, or final Shopify Plus compatibility.

## Recommended content principles

1. Start major product and guide pages with a short definition that names the product or workflow and its Shopify outcome.
2. Explain the boundary alongside the benefit: checkout logic is not storefront communication; a draft order is not a payment processor; a request form is not an approval desk.
3. Keep prices, trials, limits, and availability visible on the product page. Never turn unverified local billing configuration into a claimed App Store offer.
4. Use the resources as neutral planning guides and product pages as evaluation pages. This prevents quantity-break and quote-workflow queries from competing with their product pages.
5. Keep FAQ content visibly useful. It is not included solely to pursue FAQ rich results; Google has restricted that appearance for ordinary commercial sites.
6. Add original operational detail only where the product source or Shopify documentation supports it. Do not borrow competitor metrics, review counts, or compatibility language.
